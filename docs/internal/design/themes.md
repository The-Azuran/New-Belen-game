# Themes & Tone

Design philosophy for the game's aesthetic and narrative approach.

---

## Setting: 1990s Suburban America

### Why the 1990s?

- Pre-internet era: door-to-door evangelism was more common
- Nostalgic for many players
- Specific cultural references (Beanie Babies, Tamagotchi in library)
- Less cynical era for religious outreach
- Distinct visual/audio aesthetic

### Visual Markers

- CRT terminal aesthetic (console game)
- Retro dark theme (web version)
- Period-appropriate store names (Piggly Wiggly, Kwik-E-Mart)
- Suburban street names (Oak, Maple, Main)

---

## Narrative Philosophy

### Emergent Over Scripted

The story is not pre-written. It emerges from:
- Player choices
- Random NPC personalities
- Success/failure patterns
- Hidden mechanics discovered

### Personal Over Generic

Every playthrough should feel unique:
- NPCs have generated names
- Locations are procedural
- Memory system tracks specific encounters
- Journal reflects individual experience

### Subtle Over Explicit

The game trusts players to feel:
- Internal monologue is brief, evocative
- No explicit morality system
- Satanic path discovered, not handed to player
- Endings based on actions, not dialogue choices

## Tone Balance

### Humor

- Character descriptions (Titi Olga "such a blessing")
- Prosperity gospel satire (Reverend Joel)
- 1990s references
- NPC personality quirks

### Sincerity

- Belen's backstory (genuine religious journey)
- Seeker NPCs (authentic spiritual searching)
- Hunger/exhaustion mechanics (real sacrifice)
- Memory system (player actions matter)

### Darkness

- Hidden Satanic path
- Resistant NPCs (some people can't be "saved")
- Reputation spirals (neighborhood hostility)
- Exhaustion endings (body gives out)

## Character Design

### Protagonist Diversity

- Belen Torres: Dominican, elderly, female, ex-witch
- Dr. Scott Johnson: Academic, intellectual
- Sister Joyce Meyer: Energetic, female
- Pastor Billy Graham Jr.: Famous name, legacy
- Reverend Joel Prosperity: Satirical, prosperity gospel
- Brother Marcus: Street preacher, humble
- Titi Olga: Community mother, ambiguously manipulative

### NPC Archetypes

Based on real door-to-door experience:
- **Skeptic**: Intellectually dismissive
- **Seeker**: Genuinely curious
- **Lonely**: Wants connection, not theology
- **Busy**: No time, real constraint
- **Hostile**: Past negative experiences
- **Devout Other**: Already religious
- **Intellectual**: Enjoys debate
- **Cynic**: Distrusts institutions

---

## Moral Ambiguity

The game does not tell players what to think:

### Is preaching good?
- Conversion scored positively
- But aggressive tactics damage reputation
- Some NPCs "can't be converted" (resistant)

### Is the Satanic path evil?
- Higher conversion rate (mechanically rewarded)
- Hidden, requires failure to discover
- Special victory condition
- No explicit moral judgment

### Is persistence virtue or harassment?
- Returning to failed NPCs allowed
- Reputation system tracks repeated failures
- Neighborhoods can become hostile
